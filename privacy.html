Privacy Policy — Journal with Will

Effective 29 August 2026 · first published 21 August 2026

Journal with Will is a journaling app made by one person. This policy is short because the honest answer is short: the app collects nothing, and it never sends what you write anywhere. It stays on your phone unless you deliberately share it yourself.

What the app collects

Nothing. Journal with Will has no accounts, no login, no analytics, no advertising, and no servers. We never see your name, your email address, your writing, or anything else. There is nothing to see, because there is nowhere for it to arrive.

There is no server of ours for the app to talk to, and no code in it that could reach one: the app makes no network requests of any kind and needs no connection to work.

What you write leaves your phone only if you send it yourself. The app can turn an entry into a postcard, a single page, or a whole volume as a PDF, and hand that to the phone’s own share sheet — where you choose what happens next. Where it goes after that is governed by whatever you picked, not by us. Nothing is ever shared unless somebody chooses to share it. (This section said what you write “goes nowhere at all” until 24 August 2026, which had stopped being true when sharing was added.)

What stays on your device

Everything the app remembers is stored on your iPhone: your journal entries, the name and look of your companion, which souvenirs and items you have collected, your ink balance, the days you have written, and your settings. Deleting the app deletes all of it from that device.

Backups

The app does not sync anything to iCloud, and it has no account, so there is nothing of yours anywhere but the device in your hand.

Your device's own iCloud backup, if you have that switched on in iOS Settings, includes this app's data along with everything else on the phone. That is Apple's backup of your whole device, run by Apple under Apple's privacy terms — it is not something this app sends, and the developer has no access to it or any way to read it. Restoring a new phone from that backup restores what you had here.

If you set a new phone up from scratch rather than from a backup, the app starts fresh. Nothing is recoverable, because nothing was ever stored anywhere else.

The microphone

Journal with Will does not use the microphone. This version does not ask for microphone or speech permission, and cannot: the app declares neither, so iOS would refuse it.

If a future version adds dictation, it will ask you first, this policy will say exactly what it does before that version ships, and the audio will be transcribed on your own phone and discarded rather than stored or sent anywhere.

Notifications

If you choose a daily writing reminder, it is scheduled locally on your device by iOS. Nothing about it leaves your phone. You can change the time or turn it off at any moment in the app's Settings.

The app only asks for notification permission after you have chosen a reminder time — never at first launch, and never on a new device where a reminder time arrived.

What the app never records

Absence. The app is built so that time away is not a failure, and it follows that nothing anywhere — on the device or on any screen — keeps a count of the days you did not write. There is no such number to collect, because none is ever written down. The same is true of the day's writing prompt: nothing records whether you used it, ignored it, or asked for another.

Children

Journal with Will is rated 4+. It shows no ads, has no chat, no purchases, and collects no data from anyone — including children.

There are three links out of the app, all on the About screen. Two go to pages we publish and open in the phone’s browser: “Read the full privacy policy” opens this page, and “Help and answers” opens the app’s support page. The third is our email address, which opens the phone’s mail app with a message already addressed to us — nothing is sent unless you write it and send it yourself. There is nowhere else in the app that a child, or anyone, can be taken.

This said two links and ended “there is nowhere else” until 29 August 2026, and the third — the email row — had been on that same screen the whole time. It is a small undercount and it is corrected here for the reason the miscount matters: the review notes submitted alongside this policy say three, so the two documents Apple reads would have contradicted each other.

Changes

If a future version of the app ever changes any of the above, this policy will be updated first, and the change will be described in plain language in the App Store release notes.
